# 04 — Copy, story and brand narrative for North

Persona: copy / story / brand narrative strategist. Question: what story does the interface tell, in what voice, with how few words. Founder's hypothesis: "less telling gives more feeling."

## 1. Evidence: does "less telling" convert and feel premium?

**Verdict: mostly yes, with one correction.** Short copy wins when it is *concrete*; it loses when it is *vague*. The data punishes difficult words and reading time, not information.

- Unbounce 2024 Conversion Benchmark (41K landing pages, 57M conversions): pages at 5th–7th-grade reading level convert at 11.1%, "professional" level at 5.3%. Difficult words (3+ syllables) correlate −24.3% with conversion, word count −18.6%, reading time −19.4%. Only exception: financial services, where more complex language helps slightly. Mechanism: *easier*, not *emptier*.
- Nielsen & Morkes (NN/g, 1997): rewriting a page concise (+58% measured usability), scannable (+47%) and objective, no hype (+27%) gave +124% combined. Objective beat promotional language: "telling" in the marketing sense hurts; stating facts does not.
- NN/g tone-of-voice study (100 adults, 4 brand pairs): trustworthiness explains ~52% of willingness to recommend; friendliness only ~8%. The playful insurance copy was rated friendlier but *less* trustworthy. Casual, serious, moderately enthusiastic won. Premium is a trust feeling, and trust comes from matter-of-fact copy.
- Long vs short A/B tests are split: MECLABS shortest variant +54%; Highrise shorter page +102%; but a 1,500→4,000-word sales page +96% and NextAfter long-form ads +42%. The variable that reconciles them is Schwartz's awareness level and the stakes of the decision: unaware or high-stakes buyers need the argument; aware buyers need the fact and the button.
- Julian Shapiro's working formula: Purchase rate = Desire − (Labor + Confusion). Short copy lowers labor; vague short copy raises confusion. Harry Dry's test: "Would this help me sell if I met the customer in person?" and "a caveman should glance at it and grunt back what you offer."
- Luxury confirms the restraint side: Kapferer & Bastien's anti-laws ("forget about positioning, luxury is not comparative", "do not respond to rising demand", "don't pander to your customer's wishes"). Hermès' homepage today: no comparison, no discount, no superlatives, mood sentences of 5–12 words. Apple's homepage today: nine product headlines of 2–10 words ("Pro further." "A battery you can't outrun." "Designed for life at home."), each one concrete claim, no adjective stacks. Even Apple slips ("Now supercharged by M4"); North should not.

**Decision rule for North.** North's first users are technical builders: solution-aware, and the stakes are high (money, their name, their code). So:
1. Headline and section claims: *few words, each concrete* (a number, a noun, a mechanism). Never an abstract benefit ("elevate your business").
2. Where risk lives (money, email sent in your name, deploy, data), *explain more*, in plain words, close to the button. That is the one place a longer sentence earns its keep.
3. Measure: no sentence over 20 words on a marketing page, over 15 in the app; reading level grade 7 or below (Flesch-Kincaid); zero three-syllable marketing adjectives.

## 2. North's story (Raskin / StoryBrand form)

- **Old world:** A solo founder with a job runs the company in the gaps: a hundred suggestions a day, tools that wait for you, work instead of results.
- **The shift:** Agents can now research, build, market and sell on their own; what they cannot do is decide for you.
- **The enemy:** Tools that do nothing while you sleep, and tools that do everything without asking.
- **The promise:** Your company runs all day; you make at most three decisions, with a safe default if you make none.
- **The proof:** A ledger of every action, what it changed and what it cost; evidence on every finished task; your code in your GitHub; money only from your accounts; one button that stops everything in five seconds.
- **The call:** Start a company. Fourteen days, no card.

Duarte's "what is / what could be" rhythm maps onto the landing page: each section states the old world in one clause and the new in the next ("Yesterday you had forty tabs. Today you have one brief."). Godin's "people like us do things like this": people like us *decide*; we do not babysit tools.

## 3. Landing page copy system

**Hero (headline ≤12 words, subline ≤25).**

A. *Be the CEO of your life. Let the staff be software.* (10) — current line; emotional, keep as the brand line.
Subline: *State a thesis, a goal and a budget. Eight agents research, build, market and sell. You answer at most three decisions a day.* (23)

B. *Three decisions a day. Everything else gets done.* (8) — Apple-grammar: number first, then the promise.
Subline: *Agents plan, build, market and sell for your company. They ask you only when money, your name or your code is on the line.* (24)

C. *A company that runs while you sleep. You still decide.* (10) — names the enemy by contrast.
Subline: *Every move an agent makes passes a gate. Small ones run. Big ones become a card with a recommendation, a safe default and a deadline.* (25)

Recommendation: test B as H1 with A as the closing line of the page. B passes the caveman test; A does not on its own (what is sold?).

**Sections (3–5), one claim each, ≤12 words:**
1. The brief — *Sixty seconds every morning: what changed, what it cost, what needs you.*
2. Cards — *A question, two options, a recommendation, a deadline. One tap.*
3. The gate — *Nothing reaches the world without a tier. Money and your name need you.*
4. The ledger — *Every action, what it changed, what it cost. Nothing is ever deleted.*
5. Stop — *One button. Every agent stops within five seconds.*

Each section: claim, one 15-word sentence, a real screenshot (Dry: "show the real product, not illustrations").

**Proof block (only what North can keep honest):** a real ledger excerpt with costs; a real decision card; "Your code stays in your GitHub"; "Money moves only from your own accounts; North never holds funds"; "Sandbox by default; live keys are yours to enter"; "Export everything, any time". No logos North does not have, no "trusted by", no invented numbers. Once there are users, one number only: cards answered, or dollars tracked in ledgers.

**CTA language.** Primary: *Start a company* (value, not action; Dry rule 8). Under it, mono: *14 days · no card · your code stays in your GitHub*. Secondary: *See a brief* (opens a sample brief). Never "Get started free!", "Join thousands", "Unlock".

**Pricing voice.** Nouns and numbers; no "unlock", "unleash", "everything you need". Pattern per plan: *Solo. $X a month. 1 company. N agent runs. Cancel any time; your data is kept 90 days.* Trial line: *No plan price during the trial* (already in the app; good). Credit pack: *100 runs, $29. Never expire.*

## 4. In-app voice rules (before → after)

Voice in one line: **a competent chief of staff reporting to the CEO.** Matter-of-fact, second person for the user, first person plural never; agents speak in the third person by role name ("Growth") or first person singular only inside their own report. Casual-serious (NN/g's winning profile), no humour in errors (NN/g, Apple).

1. **Brief headline = one fact, past tense, with a number.** *Before:* "Here's your morning update!" *After:* "2 sign-ups yesterday. $14 spent. 1 card waiting."
2. **Card question is a yes/no or A/B question under 15 words, verb first.** *Before:* "Approval needed for proposed outreach campaign initiative." *After:* "Send 5 first-contact emails from your Gmail?"
3. **Option labels are the action, not Yes/No** (Apple WWDC22: "Cancel Subscription / Keep Subscription"). *Before:* "Approve ★ / Decline". *After:* "Send 5 emails ★ / Don't send".
4. **The default line names the consequence, not the mechanism.** *Before:* "No answer by the deadline → decline". *After:* "No answer by Thu 18:00 → nothing is sent." K3: "No default. Nothing happens until you answer." (already right)
5. **Cost and deadline are numerals, always visible, never adjectives.** "$10/day · due Thu 18:00", never "a small test budget".
6. **Kill switch label is the verb and the object.** Keep "Stop all agents". Confirmation: "Stop all agents? Queue freezes, campaigns pause within 5 seconds. Cards wait." Buttons: "Stop everything / Keep running". Resume: "Resume agents".
7. **Pause banner: state, reason, what still works, what to do.** Current "All agents are stopped. Queue frozen, campaigns paused." is good. Billing: "Agents paused: subscription ended. Brief and export still work. Data kept until 14 Jan. Resubscribe to resume."
8. **Empty states say what will appear and who makes it** (Apple Podcasts pattern). *Before:* "Nothing here yet." *After:* "No ledger entries yet. The first appears when an action changes a metric."
9. **Errors: what happened, what did not happen, what next; no "oops", "invalid", "sorry".** Current "Stripe did not answer in time. Nothing was charged." is the model. *Before:* "Something went wrong; nothing was changed." *After:* "The agents could not be reached. Nothing was changed. Try again in a minute."
10. **Agent status lines are present-progressive verb phrases, ≤5 words, with the object.** Devin/Cursor pattern: "Working", "Waiting for review", "Needs attention". North: "Researching 12 competitors", "Waiting for your card", "Done · evidence attached", "Failed · see why".
11. **Ledger rows: action · metric before → after · cost · evidence link.** Same tense, same order every row: "Ads started · site_visitors 120 → 410 · $38 · campaign ↗".
12. **Confirmations are nouns, never "successfully" or "Congrats!"** (Polaris). "Card answered." "Plan saved." "Export ready."
13. **Billing speaks in the user's money, in the user's words.** *Before:* "Entitlement status: past_due". *After:* "Payment failed. Agents keep running for 3 days. Update your card."
14. **Never ask for trust without data** (Polaris). *Before:* "Trust the recommendation." *After:* "Recommended: option A. Last 3 tests at this budget returned 2–4 sign-ups each."
15. **No generalising words** ("every", "all", "always") unless literally true; "Every action passes the gate" is true, so it stays.

## 5. The "agent speaks" grammar

- **Reporting work:** past tense, first-person singular only in the agent's own report, lead with the result, then evidence, then cost. ≤2 sentences. "Shipped the landing page. Preview: url. 1 run, $0.41." Never "I've gone ahead and", never "I'd be happy to".
- **Asking for a decision:** one question, present tense, the user as "you", the thing at stake named (money, your name, your code), then the recommendation with a reason of one sentence and a number, then the default. "Start a $10/day Meta test for 5 days? Recommended: the thesis names this audience and 3 of 4 similar tests found buyers. If you don't answer by Thu 18:00, nothing starts."
- **Admitting failure:** what failed, what did not happen, what the agent will do, no apology words. "Build failed on test 14. Nothing deployed. Retrying once with the fix; if it fails again the task stops and the brief says so."
- **Stating cost:** numerals, dollars, period, cap. "$11 of $10 today" (already in the UI; keep). Never "a bit over budget".
- **Forbidden in agent speech:** "I think you'll love", "exciting", "great news", exclamation marks, emoji, "let's", "we" (there is no we; the user is the company), hedges ("might possibly"), and any claim without an evidence link.
- **Silence rule (Linear AIG: "silence leads to uncertainty"):** every running task shows a status line that changes at least every 60 seconds or says when it last changed ("Researching · 4 min").

## 6. Banned words and allowed vocabulary

**Banned (grep-able):** seamless, elevate, unlock, unleash, delve, empower, revolutionize, effortless, supercharge, game-changer, game changing, cutting-edge, next-level, leverage (verb), robust, streamline, harness, navigate (metaphorical), landscape (metaphorical), tapestry, journey (marketing sense), supercharged, AI-powered, powered by AI, magic, superpowers, 10x, "not only … but also", successfully, oops, uh-oh, invalid, congrats, awesome, amazing, exciting, "we're thrilled", "welcome aboard", emoji in UI strings, ✨, exclamation marks in system copy. Economist 2025 adds the deeper tell: polysyllabic nominalisations ("the implementation of the optimisation") where a verb would do.

**Allowed, preferred:** decide, run, plan, build, ship, send, start, stop, pause, resume, cost, spent, changed, evidence, default, deadline, gate, tier, brief, card, ledger, agent, thesis, goal, budget, cap, your GitHub, your account, nothing happened, kept, deleted (only when true: never for actions/ledger). Numerals always ("3 cards", not "three cards") except in the brand line.

## 7. Glossary names: validation

The CONTEXT.md names are mostly already Apple/Linear-grade plain nouns. Keep: **Brief, Card (Decision Card), Ledger, Evidence, Thesis, Goal, Budget, Plan, Agent, Chief of Staff, Signal, Artifact, Credit, Export, Public Page, Team.** Observations:
- **Kill Switch**: right glossary name, wrong UI word. Keep the button "Stop all agents" everywhere; "kill" is never shown to a user.
- **Tier K0–K3**: internal codes. In UI, show the meaning once per card: "Runs automatically / Tells you after / Needs your decision / Only you can do this". The K-code may sit in the mono label, never alone.
- **Staff vs agents**: the tagline says staff, the app says agents. Use *agents* in product, *staff* only in the brand line. The Team page name is right.
- **Cap Card / Connect Card / Auto Rule**: never say "cap card" to a user; the card's question carries the meaning ("Raise today's cap to $25 and resume?"). "Auto rule" → UI label "Standing answer" reads more like what it is; low priority, only if the settings screen is rebuilt.
- **Entitlement, Billing Session, Provenance, Attribution Window, Local Day**: never in UI; "Plan and credits", "Payment", "Source", "Window".
- **Outcome Ledger**: "Ledger" alone in nav is correct; "Outcome ledger" as the page title once.
- **Artifacts**: slightly technical; "Work" was considered and rejected: "Artifacts" is precise, already used by Claude, and the audience is technical. Keep.

## 8. What North should do (testable rules)

1. No sentence over 20 words on marketing pages; over 15 in app strings. (count)
2. Marketing pages ≤ grade 7 Flesch-Kincaid; app strings ≤ grade 6. (score)
3. Hero H1 ≤ 12 words and contains a number or a concrete noun (company, decisions, brief). (count)
4. Every section claim ≤ 12 words, one claim, one real screenshot. (count/inspect)
5. Zero banned-list words in `apps/web` strings and in agent prompt templates; CI grep fails the build. (grep)
6. Zero exclamation marks and zero emoji in UI strings. (grep)
7. Every number shown as a numeral with a unit ($, /day, min, %). (grep for spelled-out numbers)
8. Every card question starts with a verb and ends with "?"; ≤15 words. (regex)
9. Every option label names the action and object ("Send 5 emails"), never Yes/No/OK/Approve/Decline alone. (grep)
10. Every card shows cost and deadline as numerals before the options. (screenshot)
11. Every default line has the shape "No answer by <time> → <consequence>" or "No default. Nothing happens until you answer." (regex)
12. Every error string contains what did not happen ("Nothing was charged/sent/changed") and one next step. (review checklist)
13. No error string contains "invalid", "oops", "sorry", "please", "successfully". (grep)
14. Every empty state names what will appear and who or what creates it. (review)
15. Every agent report ≤2 sentences, result first, with an evidence link or "no evidence yet". (regex on prompt output schema)
16. Agent status lines ≤5 words, present-progressive verb + object, with a last-changed time if >60 s. (regex)
17. Ledger rows follow one column order: action · before → after · cost · evidence. (schema)
18. "Kill", "K0–K3", "entitlement", "provenance" never appear as a visible string without a plain label beside it. (grep)
19. CTA is value-phrased ("Start a company"), the same words on landing, login and empty-company state. (grep)
20. Proof block contains only claims a reviewer can verify in the repo or a screenshot: no logos, no "trusted by", no invented counts. (review)
21. Pricing copy per plan ≤ 20 words: price, companies, runs, cancellation, data retention. (count)
22. Second person "you/your" for the user; "we/our" appears zero times in app strings; agents are named by role. (grep)
23. Confirmations are nouns + past participle ("Plan saved"), ≤3 words. (regex)
24. Read-aloud test on every new string set before merge (Apple's final tip); reviewer initials in the PR. (process)
